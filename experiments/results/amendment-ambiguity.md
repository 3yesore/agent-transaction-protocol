# The Ambiguity Budget of an Amendment Specification

Generated by experiments/amendment-ambiguity/run.ts.

The meta-consensus study showed that diversity makes a self-amending chain worse once
a rule fork is expensive, and its third falsifier named the way out: an amendment
procedure with d = 0, unambiguous enough that independent clients cannot diverge. This
measures what it would take.

A specification does not have to be wrong to fork a chain. It only has to be silent.
So the decision function here is three-valued:

~~~text
valid | invalid | undetermined
~~~

**undetermined** is the ambiguity, and it is exactly where two honest clients can
disagree. The quantity is d, the fraction of amendments the specification does not
decide.

The corpus is 12 amendments covering the ways an amendment can be
defective. The specification is built from 7 clauses, each one a
decision it must make explicitly.

## 3. Encoding alone can fork a chain

Three amendments carry **identical semantic content** and differ only in their bytes:

- non-canonical-key-order: identical content, keys in a different order
- duplicate-keys: the same key appears twice on the wire
- insignificant-whitespace: canonical field order but padded with whitespace

Under a specification that does not require canonical encoding, all three are
undetermined, so one client may accept a byte sequence another rejects while both agree
on what it MEANS. At the object level that is a rejected block. At the meta level it is a
split.

## Conclusion

**1. Ambiguity is measurable and it is the right quantity.** Not correctness - the
specification can be entirely correct and still leave a third of the corpus undecided,
because it never says what happens to a malformed amendment. d is that fraction, and it
is precisely where two honest clients diverge.

**2. A total specification drives d to zero.** Stating the 7 clauses gets
there, and it does so without rejecting the conforming case, so determinacy does not come
from blanket refusal.

**3. The kernel's one rule must be written down, or the most important case is
undetermined.** With the rule implicit, a domain authorising its own replacement is not
invalid - it is undecided. Three distinct shapes (absent, wrong, self) are closed by
stating that one clause. This is the constructive counterpart to the freeze's own open
question: the only irreducible constraint is pre-state evaluation, and it is only a
constraint once it is explicit.

**4. Encoding is a fork surface, not a detail.** Three amendments with identical meaning
and different bytes are enough to split clients that agree about semantics entirely. Canon-
ical encoding is not a serialization preference; it is part of the amendment procedure.

That gives the chain a concrete design obligation rather than a hope: publish the
amendment decision procedure as a total function over a canonical encoding, ship a
conformance suite for it, and only then is diversity free again.
