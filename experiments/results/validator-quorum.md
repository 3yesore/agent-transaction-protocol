# Validator Quorum Under Correlated Failure

Generated by experiments/validator-quorum/run.ts.

ATP-0002 says selection over valid successors is a domain-level mechanism and says
nothing about whether it is safe. This measures what its safety actually depends on.

Two error sources, separated on purpose:

- a **shared cause** that hits a whole client family and flips every member (a bug in
  the shared client, an ambiguous rule, a coordinated input);
- **independent noise** at 0.15 per validator.

A validator is one that runs R and votes. Ground truth is 50/50 valid and invalid.
**False accept** is a quorum canonicalising an invalid transition - the dangerous
direction, because in ATP-0002 selection is not rolled back. **False reject** costs
only liveness.

## 3. The floor is set by the largest family

Seven validators, majority (k=4), rho = 0.2. The design rule that falls out is:

> **No client family may hold at least as many validators as the threshold.**

A family that holds k members is one shared cause away from the threshold on its own.

| families | split | max family | >= k? | P(false accept) | analytic floor |
|----------|-------|------------|-------|-----------------|----------------|

## Conclusion

The study refuted its own first hypothesis, which is the useful part.

**1. False acceptance is floored at the shared-cause rate.** Held across all 24
configurations of the first sweep. When a shared cause fires, every member of the
affected family flips and the quorum accepts regardless of its threshold.

**2. The floor is set by the largest family, not by the number of families.** This was
the surprise. Splitting seven validators at k=4 into 4+3 does not move the floor at all,
because a four-member family is still one shared cause from the threshold - and the
split is measurably WORSE (20.9% to 28.4%), because a three-member cause plus a single
independent flip now also reaches k. The design rule that follows:

> **No client family may hold at least as many validators as the threshold.**

**3. The floor is a bound, not a prediction.** It is tight only when one family can
carry the threshold by itself. Where causes must combine, independent noise fills the
shortfall: at five families two causes give 14 votes and four more have to come from
noise, so the realised risk is 13.7% against a floor of 5.8%.

**4. Diversity de-correlates; it does not reduce the marginal error rate.** At full
diversity the floor is zero but the residual is not - every validator still carries its
own family's shared cause, so the marginal error is rho + (1-rho)p, 32% here, and
reaching 18 of 35 is a binomial tail: 1.6% measured against 1.1% predicted from that
tail alone. Diversity and a low shared-cause rate are two different requirements.

**5. Threshold is not a strictness parameter.** Unanimity is far safer than majority
when validators are independent, buys almost nothing at rho = 0.3, and quadruples
false rejections.

For ATP-0002 this is a boundary result. The freeze correctly says selection is not a
kernel property, so the kernel cannot be blamed for any of it. It also means **a
chain's safety claim cannot come from ATP-0002 and cannot come from validator count or
stake. It comes from implementation diversity, which is an ecosystem property.** A
chain that ships one client has a safety floor equal to its shared-cause rate, and no
number of validator slots or staked tokens moves it.
